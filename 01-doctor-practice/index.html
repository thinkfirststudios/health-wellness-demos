Registering

What happens when you register

It takes one phone call and one form. Most people are seen within a few weeks, depending on who they choose.

  1. Call us or use the form. Tell us who you would like to see.
  2. We call you back for 10 minutes to check the practice fits: your plan, your provider, and anything we do not do.
  3. We request your records from your previous practice. You sign one release.
  4. You have a 40-minute first appointment with your provider.
  5. After that you see the same person. If they are away, the roster shows who is free.

Care here

What we do, described plainly

Each one is an appointment with a stated length.

A first appointment for a new patient

Forty minutes with your provider, going through your history, your medicines and what you want from a primary care practice.

Ongoing care for a long-term condition

Regular 20-minute follow-ups with the same provider, at a pace you agree together.

A short-notice appointment for something new

Fifteen minutes the same week, usually with Priya Raman. Call from 8:00 am.

Well-child visits and school forms, from age two

Dr Vlček measures, listens and talks with you and your child. School forms are completed during the visit.

Contraception, sexual health and travel vaccinations

A private appointment with Priya Raman. Book travel vaccinations at least six weeks before you leave.

Annual wellness and Medicare annual visits

Forty minutes reviewing your year, your records and what is due next.

More about each kind of visit

What we don't do

Knowing this now saves you a wasted appointment

If you need one of these, here is where we send you and roughly how long it takes.

Urgent or emergency care
Call 911 or go to your nearest emergency department. We will see you afterward and take over from there.
Delivering babies
Dr Okonjo shares pregnancy care with an obstetric practice you choose. We send the referral within two working days, and a first visit usually takes one to two weeks.
Surgery, or procedures beyond minor office ones
We refer you to a surgeon. Expect two to four weeks for a first appointment.
Children under two
We refer to a pediatric practice, usually within the week. We see children from age two.
Long-term controlled-substance prescribing for pain
We refer you to a specialist clinic. A first appointment usually takes four to eight weeks.
Workers' compensation or legal assessments
We can give you the name of an occupational health service. The wait is usually one to two weeks.

When you could actually be seen

The last appointment is the latest time a visit can start. A new-patient visit needs 40 minutes, so it has to start at least 40 minutes before we close.

We hold a few same-week slots each weekday for something new. They go in the order people call. Priya Raman sees most of them.

(555) 555-0142

Holidays. We close on federal holidays, and at 12:00 pm on the day before Thanksgiving and the day before Christmas. Closures are posted here two weeks ahead.

After we close. Call the main number and follow the recording to reach the on-call provider. They can advise on whether something can wait until morning. They cannot book appointments, refill prescriptions or give results by phone. Those wait until we open.

In an emergency, call 911. We do not provide emergency care.

The practice

A small practice on Candler Street

Braeburn is one practice in one building: four providers, four rooms, one front desk. First appointments are 40 minutes and follow-ups are 20. You see the same person each time, and if they are away the front desk tells you who is free.

We speak English and Spanish every day. For any other language we use a phone interpreter at no cost: just ask when you call. We are on the ground floor at 214 Candler Street, Suite 2, with parking beside the building.
