Insurance

We are in network with Ardmoor Health, Penfield Mutual and Tessaly Care. Not every clinician takes every plan, so check the entry for the person you’re interested in.

With any other plan we are out of network. We give you a monthly superbill to send to your insurer.

We can’t tell you what your plan covers. Only your insurer can do that. Call the number on your card and ask about outpatient mental health benefits.

The sliding scale

Is there a sliding scale?

Yes, for individual sessions with the clinicians marked in the list.

What is the range?

$70 to $160 for an ongoing session.

How do you ask for it?

Tell the intake coordinator, by phone or email, that you’d like the sliding scale. You don’t need to give a reason, and we don’t ask for pay stubs or tax forms.

Who decides?

The intake coordinator sets your fee on that call. Your therapist isn’t part of the conversation. You can ask to change it at any time.

What we offer

You don’t need a diagnosis, or to know what is wrong. Any of these is enough to call about.

  • I’ve been meaning to call someone since the spring.
  • We keep having the same argument.
  • From the outside everything looks fine.
  • Someone died, and everyone else has gone back to normal.
  • I go over things at night and can’t stop.
  • I’m not sure this is serious enough to call about.

Anxiety and worry

For worry that has started to take up your days: at work, at night, or around other people.

Low mood

For a long flat stretch, losing interest in things, or finding the ordinary day harder than it was.

Grief and loss

For the death of someone close, and for other losses: a marriage, a job, a pregnancy, a parent's decline.

Trauma

For the effects of something that happened to you, recently or a long time ago. You decide how much you talk about it, and when.

Couples and relationships

For two people who want a third person in the room while they talk about something that keeps going wrong.

Teenagers, 13 to 17

For teenagers who would rather talk to someone outside the family, with a parent or guardian involved in ways agreed at the start.

The first appointment

How long is it?

Sixty minutes. Ongoing sessions after that are fifty.

Who will you see?

The therapist you requested, on your own or with your partner if you’re coming as a couple. Nobody else is in the room.

What happens?

They’ll ask what made you get in touch now, what you’d like to be different, and a little about your life and health. You’ll be asked to read and sign a consent form beforehand, and they will go through what is confidential and what isn’t.

Will you have to tell the whole story on day one?

No. You can say as much or as little as you want, and you can say that you aren’t ready to talk about something.

What does it cost?

$190, or your plan’s amount if we’re in network, or the fee you agreed with the intake coordinator on the sliding scale.

What happens after?

Your therapist will tell you what they think and how often they’d suggest meeting. You don’t have to decide in the room. You can go home and call us.

What if it isn’t the right fit?

Say so, to them or to the intake coordinator. You can try someone else here or ask for names outside the practice. Nobody will ask you to justify it.

What we don’t offer

Can you prescribe?

No. None of our clinicians prescribe. With your written permission we’ll work alongside your doctor or psychiatrist.

Do you do court or custody evaluations?

No. We don’t write custody recommendations or take on court-ordered assessments.

Do you see people for substance use or eating disorders?

Not as the main focus of the work, and not where someone needs more than a weekly session. Intake will give you names of people who do this work.

Do you provide emergency or after-hours care?

No. Nobody here answers the phone after 5 p.m. or on weekends, and voicemail isn’t checked until the next working day. If you need help outside those hours, here is what to do.

Confidentiality

What you say here stays between you and your therapist. We don’t confirm to anyone that you are a client without your written permission.

There are limits, and your therapist will go through them at your first appointment. We have to act if we believe you may seriously harm yourself or someone else, if we learn that a child or an older or dependent adult is being abused or neglected, or if a court orders us to release records. If you use insurance, your insurer receives billing details and a diagnosis code.

Why there are no reviews here

We don’t publish client reviews, and we don’t ask for them. A client can’t freely say no to their own therapist, and being asked can change the work between you, so the ethics codes we practice under restrict it. If you want to know what someone is like, read what they’ve written about their work, or ask the intake coordinator.

Getting here

The front entrance is step-free and there are two accessible parking spaces beside it. There is an elevator to the second floor, and an accessible restroom on each floor. If you would rather not sit in a waiting room, there is a separate quiet room, or you can wait in your car and we’ll call you.
