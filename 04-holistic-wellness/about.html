One person, one room

I’m Wren Adaba. I work alone, on the first floor at 9 Parson Street. Here’s why the room is the way it is, how a day runs, and what I don’t do.

Why I work alone

So that the person you talk to on the phone is the person in the room. There’s no reception and nobody else’s schedule. If you’ve told me something once, you won’t have to tell it to anyone else.

Why the room is set up this way

The two chairs come first, because every first appointment starts with a conversation, and that’s easier sitting down with your shoes on. The table stands clear of the wall so I can walk around it. There’s one lamp and no overhead light. Nothing in the room is for sale.

How a day runs

I see one person at a time. Between appointments I open the window, change the sheet and write my notes. Tuesday to Friday my first appointment starts at 9:00 am and my last starts at 6:00 pm. Saturdays finish earlier, and that’s when the table folds away for the breathwork group of six.

If I don’t pick up, I’m with someone. Leave a message and I’ll call back the same day.

What I don’t do

  • I don’t diagnose anything.
  • I don’t treat conditions.
  • I don’t replace or comment on your medical care.
  • I don’t prescribe or sell anything.
  • I don’t recommend a course of sessions.
  • I don’t see anyone under 16.

I don’t replace or comment on your medical care. If you’re under a doctor’s care, tell them what you’re doing here.

Who I see

People aged 16 and over. Anyone under 18 needs a parent or guardian’s signed consent at the first appointment.

Languages

I speak English and Armenian. If you’d like an interpreter for another language, tell me when you book and I’ll arrange one.
