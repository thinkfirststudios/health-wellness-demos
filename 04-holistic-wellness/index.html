What happens, minute by minute

This is a first appointment for acupuncture, the one people are most nervous about. Do you undress? How many needles? Can you drive home? It’s all here, in order.

The other three are written down the same way:

Acupuncture

first appointment 80 min · after that 60 min

with Wren Adaba

  1. Minute 00

    You arrive and sit down in the room with your shoes on. Nothing happens to you for the first twenty minutes except a conversation: what you’ve tried, what you want to ask, what you’d rather not have done.

  2. Minute 20

    You lie down on the table. You keep your underwear on and roll your sleeves and pant legs up. There’s a sheet, and Wren steps out while you settle.

  3. Minute 25

    Thirty to forty very fine needles, in your back, legs and hands. Most people feel a dull heaviness, then nothing. You lie still for twenty-five minutes, and Wren is in the next room if you call.

    If it is sharp, say so, and it is moved.

  4. Minute 55

    The needles come out. You sit up slowly. Some people feel light-headed for a minute or two; you stay until you don’t.

  5. Minute 75

    You can drive. You pay at the desk. Nobody will call you afterward unless you ask us to.

How this is regulated

Two of the four things we do are regulated by your state. Two are not. Here is what that means for you, wherever you see someone. How to check a register.

Acupuncture

A licensed profession in most states, with its own board and its own rules about titles. Ask to see a license. Your state board publishes a register, and you can check a name on it in a minute.

Massage

Licensed or registered in most states, by a different body, with different requirements. The same goes here: ask, and check.

Herbal and dietary consultation, and breathwork

Not licensed professions. In practice that means there is no register to check, so ask what training the person has and who gave it, and judge it yourself.

We’d rather you checked than took our word for it.

What we don’t do

  • We don’t diagnose anything.
  • We don’t treat conditions.
  • We don’t replace or comment on your medical care.
  • We don’t prescribe or supply herbs or supplements.
  • We don’t write dietary plans.
  • We don’t see anyone under 16.

What we’ll tell you to do instead

If something is new, getting worse or worrying you, call your doctor first. If it’s an emergency, call 911.

We don’t replace or comment on your medical care. If you’re under a doctor’s care, tell them what you’re doing here.

If we think something you’ve described needs someone else, we’ll say so and stop.

Getting here

We’re at 9 Parson Street, first floor: one flight of sixteen stairs up from the street, above the frame shop. The street door is the dark one to the left of the shop window, and it’s unlocked during opening hours.

The 14 bus stops on Parson Street, a two-minute walk from the door. Parking is in the Marl Street lot, two streets away, which has accessible spaces. There’s no parking on Parson Street itself.

There are sixteen stairs and no elevator. If stairs are a problem, read this first.
