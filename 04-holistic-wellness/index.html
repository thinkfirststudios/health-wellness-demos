What happens, minute by minute

This is a first appointment for acupuncture, the one people are most nervous about. Do you undress? How many needles? Can you drive home? It’s all here, in order.

Acupuncture

first appointment 80 min · after that 60 min

  1. Minute 00

    You arrive and sit down in the room with your shoes on. Nothing happens to you for the first twenty minutes except a conversation: what you’ve tried, what you want to ask, what you’d rather not have done.

  2. Minute 20

    You lie down on the table. You keep your underwear on and roll your sleeves and pant legs up. There’s a sheet, and I step out while you settle.

  3. Minute 25

    Thirty to forty very fine needles, in your back, legs and hands. Most people feel a dull heaviness, then nothing. You lie still for twenty-five minutes, and I’m just outside the door if you call.

    If it is sharp, say so, and it is moved.

  4. Minute 55

    The needles come out. You sit up slowly. Some people feel light-headed for a minute or two; you stay until you don’t.

  5. Minute 75

    You can drive. You pay me before you go. I won’t call you afterward unless you ask me to.

How this is regulated

Acupuncture is regulated state by state. Breathwork generally isn’t. Here is what that means for you, wherever you go. How to check a register.

Acupuncture

A licensed profession in most states, with its own board and its own rules about titles. Ask to see a license. Your state board publishes a register, and you can check a name on it in a minute.

Breathwork

Generally not a licensed field, though rules differ from state to state. Where there is no register to check, ask what training the person has and who gave it, and judge it yourself.

I’d rather you checked than took my word for it.
