How this is regulated

Two of the four things we do are regulated by your state, and two are not. Most places don’t tell you that. It’s worth knowing before you see anyone, here or anywhere else.

Acupuncture

A licensed profession in most states, with its own board and its own rules about titles. Ask to see a license. Your state board publishes a register, and you can check a name on it in a minute.

Massage

Licensed or registered in most states, by a different body, with different requirements. The same goes here: ask, and check.

Herbal and dietary consultation, and breathwork

Not licensed professions. In practice that means there is no register to check, so ask what training the person has and who gave it, and judge it yourself.

We’d rather you checked than took our word for it.

How to check a register

  • Ask the person for the full name on their license and the state that issued it. Anyone who holds one can tell you.
  • Search for your state’s name and the profession, followed by the words license lookup. Use the result that ends in .gov or your state’s official domain.
  • Type in the name. The register shows whether the license is current and whether anything has been recorded against it.
  • Rules differ from state to state, including which titles a person may use, so check the state you’re in.

What to ask when there’s no register

  • What training do you have, and who gave it?
  • How long did it take, and did anyone assess your work?
  • What will you not do, and when would you tell me to see someone else?

If the answers are vague, that’s an answer too.

About dietary advice

In most states, individual nutrition advice is restricted to licensed or registered dietitians, and the rules vary. That’s why our herbal and dietary consultation is a conversation with a written summary. It isn’t a dietary plan, and nobody here will tell you what to eat.

About herbs and supplements

We don’t prescribe, supply or sell them, and we don’t make claims about them. If you’re thinking about taking something, talk to your doctor or pharmacist.

Why we publish this

You’re trusting someone with your body or your time, and you should be able to find out who they are. We’d rather you checked than took our word for it.
